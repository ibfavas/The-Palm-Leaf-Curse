### Chapter 2: The First Page

The scan fills your screen: a long, darkened palm leaf, its edges worn smooth by generations of hands. Fine lines of Kolezhuthu cross the surface, cut into the leaf with an iron stylus and blackened with soot and oil. In the hard blue light, the grooves gleam for a moment, then sink back into the brown.

You imagine the original under the brass lamp in the archive's manuscript room. The room is cold even in May, the air damp with old wood and oil. Outside, monsoon rain worries at the roof. The stream of water against your window never breaks.

Meera's message arrives: “I wish I could do something for you, too. Please make yourself some tea, at least.” You look at the cup beside your keyboard, gone cold. “I'll make another,” you tell her. Her reply comes almost at once: “Good. I can worry about your tea for a minute, then.”

The leaf holds its silence on the screen. Rain softens the room around you. Why did Raghavan stay with it until the end?
