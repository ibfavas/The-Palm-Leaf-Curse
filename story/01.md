### Chapter 1: The Professor's Desk

The State Archives shine in the hard blue light of your monitor. In Thrissur, nine days of monsoon have darkened the walls and filled the gutters; damp gathers along the window frame. On the archive site, every line is straight, every photograph carefully framed, as if paper and glass could keep the past orderly.

Professor Raghavan Menon sits in his staff portrait at a desk crowded with ledgers and tied palm leaves. One hand rests beside an open book. Rain whitens the window behind him. He looks neither toward the camera nor away from it, caught in the pause between one thought and the next. You hear Meera again: he was working when they found him.

His catalogue record names MS-1794-047, the Vadakkumkara grandham: 34 leaves. A red mark crosses the entry: **DO NOT DIGITISE.** The warning is so neatly printed that it seems less like an instruction than a fear someone tried to make official.

Meera's name lights your phone. “Are you still there? Sorry. I keep checking. It helps to know someone else is awake.” You tell her you are. A moment later, she writes, “Thank you. I know this is a lot to ask of someone.”

A power dip dims the monitor. For a breath, the professor's portrait is only your reflection laid over his. The archive gives no reason for the warning. The red words hold their place in the blue light. Who were they meant to frighten?
