### Epilogue: The Truth

You send everything to the police: the confession, the priest’s letter, the emails, the photograph taken from outside your window, and the professor’s last note.

He wrote the note for Meera, believing she would have time to read it. The last leaf was blank when Raghavan found it. It is not blank now. In the scribe’s hand, one line reads:

> “The fourth reader will finish what I started.”

Devan Kunnathu is arrested. The fixer is identified. The deed is challenged in court, the township project stalls, and the university authenticates the manuscript. For the first time in fifty years, the leaves are placed on public display.

The last line of the manuscript, in Kunjiraman’s own hand, reads:

> “I write this so that the truth outlives the men who buried it.”

A week later, your phone lights up. Meera’s number.

“thank you.”

Meera is dead. Nobody can explain the message. The police say her phone was in an evidence locker when it was sent.

In the archives, the manuscript sits in a glass case. The archivist swears the leaves rustle at night, when the building is empty and the doors are locked.

You never open the manuscript again.
