### Chapter 4: The Silence

At 1:14 a.m., Meera’s message arrives: “There’s a car outside my house. It’s been there for an hour. The lights are off.” A second text tries to make it ordinary: “I’m probably being dramatic. I had tea and I’m fine. Stay with me a little longer?”

You answer. The message is delivered. Meera does not read it.

For twenty-four hours, nothing. You call once; it rings into silence. You call again, then hang up before voicemail. Morning comes without sleep. Rain keeps the street outside your window empty and silver.

By noon, you have worn a path between the window and the desk. Her last message stays bright on your phone. No typing bubble comes back. You keep the phone face up, waiting for her name to appear.
